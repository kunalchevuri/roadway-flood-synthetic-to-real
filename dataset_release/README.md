# Roadway Flood Dataset (synthetic)

1005 synthetic roadway-flood images at three severity levels, generated by
generative image editing of real traffic-camera frames. Released alongside the paper
listed under Citation below.

## Contents

| | |
|---|---|
| Images | 1005 PNG, RGB |
| Base camera views | 335 |
| Water levels per view | 3 (every view appears at all three) |
| `no flood` | 335 |
| `small flood` | 335 |
| `big flood` | 335 |
| Resolutions | variable; most common 1920x1080 (693 images) |
| Uncompressed size | ~2.6 GB |

Images are split across two archives purely because of a download size limit. Extract
both into the same directory; filenames do not collide.

- `fixed_synthetic_output-20261007T215927Z-1-001.zip` - 730 images
- `fixed_synthetic_output-20261007T215927Z-1-002.zip` - 275 images
- `labels.csv` - label and resolution for every file

## Naming

`NNNx.png`, where `NNN` is the base camera view and `x` is the water level:

| suffix | water condition | label |
|---|---|---|
| `w` | wet pavement | `no flood` |
| `s` | shallow standing water | `small flood` |
| `l` | deep standing water | `big flood` |

`001w.png`, `001s.png` and `001l.png` are therefore the same camera view at three
water levels. Camera pose, road geometry, roadside structures and vehicle placement
are held fixed across the three, so they differ mainly in how much standing water is
present. All three images of a view share a resolution.

Note that `no flood` means **wet pavement, not a dry road**. Separating `no flood`
from `small flood` is harder than separating a dry road from a flooded one, which is
deliberate.

## Known limitations

These are generative edits, so they vary in more than water level. Lighting and sky
change visibly, and fine text such as agency logos is garbled. A model trained on
these images alone therefore has access to shortcuts that will not exist on real
footage. Treat this set as training material to be mixed with real imagery, not as a
substitute for it, and evaluate on real photographs.

## Generation

The edits were produced in January 2026 with **Gemini 3 Pro Image**, the model Google
released under the name **Nano Banana Pro** on 20 November 2025. Each water level used
its own prompt, reproduced verbatim below.

**Wet pavement (`w`, no flood)**

> Edit this image to show a wet road immediately following heavy rain. The asphalt is
> completely saturated, appearing as a dark, glossy, slick black surface. There is no
> significant water accumulation, with tires sitting cleanly on the pavement and only
> a few scattered, micro-puddles visible. All street curbs are totally dry and
> exposed. The lighting is gloomy and overcast. [GEOMETRIC ANCHOR]: Do not move, add,
> or delete any vehicles, buildings, or people. Maintain the exact original camera
> angle, focal length, and perspective. Distorted specular reflections on the slick
> road.

**Shallow standing water (`s`, small flood)**

> Edit this image to show minor, patchy street flooding. The asphalt road is a mix of
> exposed wet pavement and large, shallow puddles of standing water. The water is only
> 2 inches deep, barely touching the bottom of the tires and leaving all street curbs
> clearly visible and unsubmerged. [GEOMETRIC ANCHOR]: Do not move, add, or delete any
> vehicles, buildings, or people. Maintain the exact original camera angle, focal
> length, and perspective. Calm, mirror-like reflections in the puddles.

**Deep standing water (`l`, big flood)**

> Edit this image to show a severe urban flash flood. The gray asphalt of the road has
> been completely transformed into a deep, turbulent, murky brown floodwater. The
> water is 2 feet deep, deeply submerging the lower half of the tires of the existing
> vehicles and overflowing the curbs. [GEOMETRIC ANCHOR]: Do not move, add, or delete
> any vehicles, buildings, or people. Maintain the exact original camera angle, focal
> length, and perspective. Realistic ripples and reflections.

The `[GEOMETRIC ANCHOR]` instruction, present in all three prompts, is what keeps
camera pose, road geometry, roadside structures and vehicle placement fixed across the
three water levels of a given scene.
